## 1. Under the Lens

### STRATEGIC CHOICES FOR MAJOR POWERS THROUGH THE LENS OF THE ANCIENT CHINESE DISCOURSE ON THE SIX STATES
**By Scott Zhijiang Zhang**[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span)

When studying the power structures of modern global multipolar games, policymakers in Washington often tend to rely on Western realist theories. However, to truly gain insight into the underlying behavioral logic of the major powers across the Eurasian continent, one must understand the strategic culture deeply rooted in their civilizational bloodlines. Su Xun (1009-1066 CE), an outstanding strategist of the Northern Song Dynasty, left behind a geopolitical assertion transcending a millennium in his classic political essay *Discourse on the Six States* (六国论)—which analyzed the geopolitical collapse of the late Warring States period: *"The destruction of the six states was not due to unfavorable weaponry or poor combat, but lay in bribing the Qin."* (六国破灭,非兵不利,战不善,弊在赂秦)[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span).

From an objective geostrategic perspective, the systemic thinking revealed by this political essay is not only a summary of history, but also a striking mirror of the intricate relations among today's seven major powers: the United States, Russia, China, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, and Japan[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span).

## 2. Music-Painting-Philosophy

### FROM ROMAN HISTORY TO THE VOICES OF THE SOUL
**By Alice Jens**[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span)

> *Inter arma enim silent leges* — Cicero[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span)

Not long ago, during my sharing and discussion on classical music appreciation, I never expected it to spark a series of thoughtful ripples touching upon classical music, ancient Greek philosophy, and famous paintings of Roman history. Leon, the younger brother of my dear Greek friend Nick, reached out to me following this topic. Building upon the conversation, he shared a famous quote by the ancient Roman thinker Cicero: *Inter arma enim silent leges* (In the presence of weapons, laws fall silent)[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).

The work recommended today as a continuation of this spirit in modern times is the classic ending theme **Now We Are Free** from the Hollywood film *Gladiator*, jointly composed by score master Hans Zimmer and vocalist Lisa Gerrard[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span)[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span).
